# Pakistan Resolve --- Product Vision & Website Build Brief

**Version:** 1.0\
**Prepared:** 15 October 2026\
**Purpose:** This document is the primary product brief for an AI agent
designing and building the Pakistan Resolve website. Treat it as the
source of truth for the project's purpose, positioning, information
architecture, content model, visual direction, and MVP priorities.

**Current live prototype:**
https://pakistan-resolve.mehunhasnain.workers.dev/

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Executive summary

Pakistan Resolve is an independent Pakistan-focused public knowledge
project. Its purpose is to help people understand how Pakistan actually
works: its systems, institutions, public services, economy, energy,
politics, society, and the decisions that shape everyday life.

The project began with a broader "news and awareness information agency"
idea: a lighter, more accessible alternative to television news, focused
on civic awareness, current issues, and useful information. The vision
has since matured. The strongest long-term direction is not to become
another news page that reacts to the day's headlines. It is to build a
**structured, accessible knowledge hub about Pakistan's systems**---a
growing public databank that explains what exists, how it works, who is
responsible, where money and resources flow, what the data says, what is
failing, and what could improve.

The central idea is:

> **Pakistan Resolve turns scattered information about Pakistan into
> clear, structured public understanding.**

The tagline associated with the project is **"Awareness for Resolve."**
Awareness is the starting point; better understanding is the immediate
goal. The project should not promise that a website alone can solve
national problems. Instead, it should make the country's systems easier
to understand, question, discuss, and eventually improve.

The website should feel like a combination of: - a public knowledge
library; - a systems-explainer platform; - a civic information
resource; - a research-backed editorial publication; - and, over time, a
searchable databank about Pakistan.

It should **not** feel like a generic blog, a partisan political page, a
government portal, a cluttered newspaper homepage, or a startup landing
page with grand claims but little substance.

## 2. The problem Pakistan Resolve addresses

Information about Pakistan is spread across news reports, government
websites, regulatory documents, research papers, reports, statistics,
social media posts, and informal explanations. It can be difficult for
an ordinary reader to connect those pieces into a coherent picture.

News often explains an event without explaining the underlying system. A
headline may report a rise in electricity prices, a petrol-price change,
a tax measure, a public-sector controversy, or a new policy. But readers
may still not know: - Which institutions make the relevant decisions? -
What is the process behind the decision? - Where does the money go? -
Which rules or contracts shape the outcome? - What data is reliable? -
Who benefits, who pays, and who is accountable? - What has changed over
time? - Which claims are facts, interpretations, or political rhetoric?

Pakistan Resolve should help bridge that gap. It should make complex
systems understandable without flattening them into misleading
oversimplifications.

### The core user problem

"I hear about Pakistan's problems every day, but I struggle to
understand the machinery behind them, how the pieces connect, and where
I can find trustworthy explanations."

### The product response

Create concise, understandable entry points, then allow curious readers
to go deeper through linked explainers, sources, data, timelines,
institutional profiles, and related topics.

## 3. Mission, vision, and positioning

### Mission

To make Pakistan's systems and public issues understandable through
clear explanations, organized knowledge, and transparent sourcing.

### Long-term vision

To build a living, searchable knowledge hub about Pakistan: a public map
of how the country functions across institutions, economy, energy,
public finance, governance, society, and everyday services.

### Positioning statement

**Pakistan Resolve is an independent knowledge and awareness platform
that explains Pakistan's systems---not just the news about them.**

### Tagline

**Awareness for Resolve.**

Use the tagline as a concise brand line, not as a substitute for
explaining the product.

### Brand promise

A visitor should leave a Pakistan Resolve page understanding something
more clearly than before. The site should help people move from: -
headlines to context; - confusion to a mental model; - isolated facts to
connected systems; - unsupported claims to sourced evidence; - passive
awareness to informed questions.

### What "Resolve" means

"Resolve" refers to the intention to understand problems seriously and
create the conditions for better decisions. It does not mean that the
project claims to have immediate answers to every national problem. The
name should communicate constructive seriousness, not empty patriotism
or aggressive political rhetoric.

## 4. Evolution of the idea: preserve the useful parts of the old vision

The earlier idea positioned Pakistan Resolve as a **news and awareness
information agency**---a lighter alternative to television news, with
civic, CSS/PCS/current-affairs, and public-awareness content. That
origin remains useful. The project should still be accessible, relevant
to current issues, and capable of publishing short-form explainers.

However, the new direction should give the project a more durable
foundation.

### Keep from the old idea

-   Clear public-interest information about Pakistan.
-   A more accessible alternative to noisy, sensational news coverage.
-   Civic awareness and current-affairs context.
-   Short videos and articles that explain important issues.
-   Relevance to students, citizens, and people preparing for
    competitive examinations.
-   A recognizable independent identity.

### Evolve beyond the old idea

-   Do not make daily news publishing the core product.
-   Do not compete on speed with established newsrooms.
-   Do not make the homepage a stream of undifferentiated posts.
-   Do not let every topic exist as an isolated article with no
    connection to other knowledge.
-   Do not use "awareness" as a vague label for any opinion or viral
    content.

### The new organizing principle

**Systems first, news second.**

News and current events can be entry points into the knowledge hub. When
a major event occurs, Pakistan Resolve can explain the underlying system
and link to the relevant evergreen page. The lasting asset is the
structured explanation, not merely the timely post.

For example, an electricity-price story should connect to the wider
electricity system: generation, fuel costs, transmission, distribution
companies, billing, subsidies, capacity payments, regulation, public
finance, and the consumer's bill. A petrol-price story should connect to
the fuel supply chain, pricing formula, taxes and levies, exchange
rates, international prices, and relevant institutions. These are
examples of the intended explanatory method, not claims that all such
pages already exist.

## 5. Principles that should guide every product decision

1.  **Clarity before cleverness.** Visitors should understand what the
    site is and where to begin within seconds.
2.  **Systems over isolated headlines.** Explain relationships,
    incentives, processes, and institutions.
3.  **Evidence over confidence.** Cite sources, distinguish facts from
    analysis, and state uncertainty where it exists.
4.  **Accessible without being shallow.** Use plain language, but do not
    remove important nuance.
5.  **Independent and non-partisan.** Evaluate claims and institutions
    on evidence rather than party loyalty.
6.  **Constructive, not blindly optimistic.** Identify failures honestly
    and describe realistic options without pretending every issue has a
    simple fix.
7.  **Pakistan-specific.** Ground explanations in Pakistan's actual
    institutions, laws, geography, data, and circumstances.
8.  **Connected knowledge.** Pages should link to related systems,
    institutions, terms, datasets, and explainers.
9.  **Build incrementally.** A small, accurate library is better than a
    huge, shallow database.
10. **Respect the reader's time.** Offer a quick explanation first and
    deeper layers for readers who want them.
11. **Transparency.** Show publication/update dates, references,
    methodology, and corrections where relevant.
12. **Durability.** Prefer pages that remain useful for months or years,
    with dates and versioning for facts that change.

## 6. Audience

The website should serve multiple audiences without becoming confusing
or trying to be everything to everyone.

### Primary audiences

**Curious citizens and general readers** - Want to understand the
systems behind everyday issues. - May not have specialist knowledge. -
Need clear explanations, useful diagrams, definitions, and trustworthy
sources.

**Students and early-career learners** - Need a reliable starting point
for Pakistan-related topics. - May use the site for coursework, debate,
research, or general learning. - Benefit from summaries, key terms,
timelines, and further reading.

**CSS/PCS and current-affairs learners** - Need structured context about
governance, economy, public policy, energy, institutions, and national
issues. - Should find the site useful, but it must not become only an
exam-preparation website.

**Researchers, journalists, and engaged professionals** - Need a map of
the topic, source links, definitions, and a starting set of credible
references. - May use the site to discover relevant primary documents or
data.

### Secondary audiences

-   Pakistani diaspora seeking context.
-   Teachers, educators, and civil-society groups.
-   People who encounter Pakistan Resolve through a short video or
    social post and want a deeper explanation.

### Audience design rule

Write first for an intelligent person who is curious but has not studied
the subject. Allow the reader to go deeper without requiring every
visitor to read a long research paper.

## 8. Information architecture and navigation

The website should be organized around **topics and systems**, not
simply publication date.

### Recommended top-level navigation

1.  **Home**
2.  **Explore Systems**
3.  **Knowledge Library**
4.  **Explainers**
5.  **About**

A visible search control should be available throughout the site. "Data
& Sources" can be a top-level item later, or a section inside the
Knowledge Library until there is enough content to justify a separate
destination.

Do not overbuild navigation before there is enough material to populate
it. The architecture should support future growth while the MVP remains
focused.

### A. Home

The homepage should answer four questions quickly: - What is Pakistan
Resolve? - What can I learn here? - Where should I start? - Why should I
trust it?

Recommended homepage sequence:

1.  **Hero**
    -   Brand name: Pakistan Resolve.
    -   Short explanation: "Understand how Pakistan works."
    -   Supporting sentence explaining that the site breaks down
        Pakistan's systems, institutions, economy, energy, and public
        issues into clear, sourced explanations.
    -   Primary action: "Explore Systems."
    -   Secondary action: "Browse the Knowledge Library."
2.  **Featured system**
    -   Highlight one core system currently being developed.
    -   Initial priority: Electricity, because research and content work
        have already begun on this topic.
    -   Use a short summary, not a giant block of text.
    -   Provide a clear path into the system page.
3.  **Explore by system**
    -   A small set of topic cards, for example:
        -   Energy & Electricity
        -   Economy & Public Finance
        -   Governance & Institutions
        -   Fuel & Transport
        -   Society & Public Services
        -   History & Political Development
    -   These are proposed categories; only show a category as active
        when it has meaningful content. Empty categories should not look
        like completed libraries.
4.  **Start here / how it works**
    -   A short explanation of the Pakistan Resolve approach: understand
        the system, identify the institutions, follow the process and
        money, inspect the evidence, then explore possible explanations
        and trade-offs.
5.  **Latest explainers**
    -   A limited number of recent or newly updated pieces.
    -   Keep the section secondary to the systems/library structure.
6.  **Featured facts or data**
    -   Optional for the MVP. Only include when figures can be sourced,
        dated, and maintained.
    -   Do not put unsourced statistics in a prominent visual just to
        make the page look data-rich.
7.  **About / editorial principles**
    -   Brief statement of independence, sourcing, and purpose, with a
        link to a fuller About page.
8.  **Footer**
    -   About, editorial standards, contact, source/correction policy,
        social links if available, and copyright.

### B. Explore Systems

This should become the site's distinctive main entry point.

A system landing page should help the reader understand the system as a
whole and then navigate into its components. For each system, support:

-   **Overview:** What the system does and why it matters.
-   **How it works:** A step-by-step explanation of the process.
-   **Key institutions:** Organizations, regulators, ministries,
    companies, and other actors.
-   **Money and incentives:** Where applicable, how payments, taxes,
    subsidies, costs, and incentives flow.
-   **Rules and policies:** Relevant laws, regulations, policies, and
    formal procedures.
-   **Data and evidence:** Important figures, with dates, definitions,
    and sources.
-   **Problems and debates:** What is not working, what is contested,
    and why.
-   **Reform options and trade-offs:** Clearly labeled analysis, not
    presented as settled fact.
-   **Timeline:** Major historical changes where useful.
-   **Glossary:** Terms readers need to understand the topic.
-   **Related systems:** Links showing how this system connects to
    others.
-   **Sources and further reading:** Primary documents first where
    practical, then reputable secondary sources.

Do not force every subsection onto every system page. Use the sections
that fit the subject and available evidence.

### C. Knowledge Library

The library is the searchable, organized collection of pages and
resources.

It may eventually include: - System overviews. - Institutional
profiles. - Explainers. - Data notes and datasets. - Timelines. -
Glossary entries. - Policy and law explainers. - Research summaries. -
Source collections. - Historical backgrounders.

The library should have filters that are genuinely useful, such as
topic, content type, and possibly date. Do not add complex filtering
until enough content exists to make it useful.

Each listing should show: - Title. - A one- or two-sentence summary. -
Topic/category. - Content type, if useful. - Last updated or published
date. - Estimated reading time, if practical. - Optional cover image or
restrained icon.

### D. Explainers

Explainers are short, focused answers to questions readers actually ask.
Examples of the style of question: - How does a system work? - Why does
a bill contain these charges? - Which institution is responsible for
this decision? - What is the difference between two commonly confused
terms? - How does a policy change affect ordinary people?

An explainer should be readable on its own but link into the relevant
system page for deeper context.

### E. About

The About page should explain: - Why Pakistan Resolve exists. - The
shift from a broad news/awareness concept toward a systems-focused
knowledge hub. - What readers can expect from the platform. - Editorial
principles and approach to sources. - What the project does not claim to
be. - How readers can suggest corrections, sources, questions, or
topics. - Contact information, once a dedicated contact route is
established.

Avoid inflated claims such as "the most trusted platform" or "solving
Pakistan." Let the work establish credibility.

## 9. The core content model

The website should be designed around reusable content types rather than
treating every page as a generic blog post.

### Recommended content types

**1. System** A high-level map of a major area of public life, such as
electricity, petrol and fuel, taxation, public finance, or governance.

Suggested fields: - Title - Slug - Short description - Why it matters -
Overview - How it works - Components/stages - Key institutions - Key
terms - Important figures (each with source and date) - Main
problems/debates - Related systems - Related explainers - Sources -
Published date - Last reviewed/updated date - Editorial status (draft,
reviewed, published)

**2. Explainer** A focused article answering one clear question.

Suggested fields: - Title - Slug - Question being answered - Short
answer / summary - Main explanation - Key takeaways - Related
system(s) - Related institutions/terms - Sources - Published date - Last
updated date - Estimated reading time - Editorial status

**3. Institution** A profile of an organization or body that has a role
in a system.

Suggested fields: - Name - Type and jurisdiction -
Mandate/responsibilities - Role in relevant systems - How it is governed
or accountable, where supported - Important linked documents - Related
systems and explainers - Sources and review date

**4. Data note** A page explaining a dataset or important statistic, not
just displaying a number.

Suggested fields: - Metric/indicator - Value or series - Unit -
Geographic and time coverage - Definition and methodology - Source and
publication date - Caveats and limitations - Last updated date - Related
system and explainers

**5. Timeline** A sequence of significant, sourced events that helps
explain how a system developed.

**6. Glossary term** A concise definition, a plain-language explanation,
an example if useful, and links to relevant pages.

### Relationships are essential

The CMS/data model should allow content to link to: - systems; -
institutions; - other explainers; - data notes; - glossary terms; -
primary documents and sources.

For example, an explainer about an electricity bill could link to the
electricity system, the relevant regulator or distribution company, a
glossary entry for a tariff term, a data note, and a source document.
This connected structure is more valuable than a chronological list of
unrelated posts.

## 10. Editorial and research standards

Credibility is central to the product. The website must make sourcing
easy and avoid presenting generated or uncertain content as
authoritative.

### Source hierarchy

Where practical, prefer: 1. Primary sources: laws, official
notifications, regulator documents, government datasets, budget
documents, official reports, parliamentary records, and original
research. 2. Reputable research institutions, academic publications, and
established international organizations. 3. Credible journalism for
reporting, context, and developments. 4. Expert commentary or other
secondary material, clearly understood as interpretation.

A source's official status does not automatically make every claim in it
complete or neutral. Sources should be evaluated in context, especially
where institutions have incentives or competing interests.

### Every important factual claim should be traceable

-   Link claims to their source.
-   Include the source's publication date when relevant.
-   Give figures a time period, unit, and definition.
-   Distinguish the date a page was published from the date the
    underlying data describes.
-   Do not present an old statistic as current.
-   Explain uncertainty, disagreement, or gaps in the data.
-   Do not invent references or use citations that do not support the
    claim.

### Separate four kinds of statements

Where the distinction matters, make it clear whether a statement is: -
**Fact:** supported by identifiable evidence. - **Official position:**
what an institution or person says. - **Analysis:** an interpretation of
evidence and incentives. - **Proposal:** a possible action, with likely
trade-offs and limitations.

### Corrections and updates

Include a simple way to report an error. Important changes should be
reflected in the page's update date and, when appropriate, a short
correction note. Do not silently rewrite consequential factual claims in
a way that hides the correction.

### Avoid

-   Partisan framing presented as neutral fact.
-   Sensationalism and rage-bait.
-   Unsourced numerical claims.
-   Overconfident causal claims.
-   Copying large sections of third-party reports.
-   Treating a single report or one person's opinion as the final word
    on a complex issue.
-   Pretending a complicated system has one simple cause or one painless
    solution.

## 11. Tone and writing style

The voice should be **clear, calm, curious, independent, and
intellectually honest**.

Use: - Short paragraphs. - Specific headings framed around useful
questions. - Plain English and familiar terms. - Definitions for
technical, legal, and economic vocabulary. - Examples from everyday
Pakistani life when they illuminate the system. - Urdu terms when they
are genuinely useful, with explanation where needed. - Tables, process
diagrams, timelines, and maps when they clarify relationships. - A
concise summary at the beginning of longer pages.

Avoid: - Corporate jargon. - Empty motivational language. -
Nationalistic slogans in place of analysis. - Excessively academic
prose. - Artificially dramatic headlines. - Clickbait. - Overuse of
emojis. - Claims that the platform is "revolutionary," "unbiased" in an
absolute sense, or guaranteed to solve problems.

The content should respect readers' intelligence. Do not talk down to
them, but do not assume they know the structure of every ministry,
regulator, market, or public service.

## 12. Visual identity and user experience

### Overall direction

Use a restrained, editorial, research-oriented visual style. The site
should feel trustworthy, modern, and distinctly connected to Pakistan
without relying on decorative national symbolism everywhere.

Think: **public-interest research publication + accessible knowledge
library**, not a TV-news website or a flashy SaaS dashboard.

### Brand elements

-   Brand name: **Pakistan Resolve**
-   Tagline: **Awareness for Resolve**
-   Short brand form: **PR**, if it works clearly at small sizes.
-   Use Pakistan's green and white as meaningful brand accents, with a
    balanced neutral background and strong readable text.
-   A previously accepted logo direction harmonized the proportions of
    "Pakistan Resolve" / "PR," with Pakistan green associated with the
    "R" and "Resolve" associated with the "P." Treat that as a useful
    reference for continuity, not a requirement to force awkward
    typography.
-   If using a Pakistan map, Minar-e-Pakistan, or Badshahi Mosque motif,
    keep it restrained and legible. Do not turn the site into a collage
    of national symbols.

### Design characteristics

-   Strong typography and a clear hierarchy.
-   Generous whitespace.
-   High contrast and accessible text sizes.
-   Consistent content cards.
-   Subtle borders and restrained shadows.
-   Simple icons used consistently.
-   Charts and diagrams that explain something, not decorative data
    visualizations.
-   Responsive layouts for mobile, tablet, and desktop.
-   Fast loading and low visual clutter.
-   A clear visual difference between navigation, summaries, evidence,
    analysis, and source notes.

### Suggested visual system

Use a neutral base---white or warm off-white---with dark charcoal/ink
text and a restrained Pakistan-green accent. A secondary muted color may
be used for metadata or diagrams, but avoid a rainbow palette. The
interface should feel calm and legible.

### Accessibility and usability

-   Design mobile-first.
-   Ensure readable contrast and keyboard-friendly navigation.
-   Use descriptive link labels.
-   Add meaningful alt text to informative images.
-   Do not communicate meaning by color alone.
-   Avoid tiny text, motion-heavy interactions, and autoplaying media.
-   Make search easy to find.
-   Ensure cards and navigation have clear interactive states.
-   Use semantic headings and accessible form labels.

### Images and diagrams

Use visuals only when they add explanatory value. Prefer: - simple
system flow diagrams; - institutional relationship maps; - timelines; -
clearly labeled charts; - relevant documentary images with appropriate
rights and captions.

Do not use generic stock photography as filler on every card. Never
fabricate charts or use unsourced data to create a false sense of
authority.

## 13. Search and discovery

Search is important because the library should grow over time.

### MVP search

-   Search page titles, summaries, and main content where feasible.
-   Return useful results with clear titles, snippets, categories, and
    dates.
-   Provide a helpful empty state with suggested topics or a way to
    browse systems.
-   Include a clear route back to the system categories.

### Future discovery

-   Filter by topic, content type, institution, and time period.
-   "Related reading" based on explicit editorial links.
-   Glossary term linking and inline definitions.
-   Topic pages that gather systems, explainers, data notes, and
    institutions.
-   Cross-links from short social explainers to deeper website pages.

Do not add AI chat or an elaborate recommendation engine to the MVP. A
well-organized information architecture and reliable search are more
important.

## 14. Initial subject priorities

The platform should launch with a narrow, useful set of content rather
than pretending to cover all of Pakistan.

### First priority: Electricity

Research and content development have already begun on electricity. Make
it the initial flagship system if the existing material is ready for
publication.

A possible electricity system structure: - What the electricity system
does. - How electricity moves from generation to consumers. - Main
actors and institutions. - Generation mix and fuel sources. -
Transmission and distribution. - Distribution companies and consumer
billing. - Tariffs, taxes, surcharges, and other bill components. -
Circular debt and the financial flows behind it. - Capacity payments and
other contractual concepts, explained carefully. - Regulation and
policy. - Common problems, contested explanations, and reform debates. -
Key data, timelines, glossary, and source library.

These topics are a content roadmap, not pre-approved conclusions.
Research each claim and explain disagreements where they exist.

### Second priority: Petrol and fuel

Petrol was the first system researched for the project. Research notes
were developed around a set of core questions and intended to be turned
into several short videos. Reuse that work where appropriate, but verify
facts and sources before turning handwritten notes or scripts into
authoritative website material.

Potential structure: - How fuel reaches consumers. - The role of
international prices and exchange rates. - Pricing mechanism and review
process. - Taxes, levies, margins, and other price components. -
Relevant public and private institutions. - Supply, storage, and
distribution. - How price changes affect households, transport, and
businesses. - Historical context and sourced data. - Common
misconceptions and debates.

### Later systems

Possible future areas include: - Economy and public finance. - Taxation
and the national budget. - Governance and institutions. - Public
services. - Agriculture and water. - Transport. - Education and health
systems. - Political development and constitutional structure. - Society
and demographic change.

Do not build empty pages for every topic just to create the appearance
of scale. Make the library expand when researched material is ready.

## 15. Connection to short-form content and social media

Pakistan Resolve is also intended to publish accessible short videos and
social content. The website and social channels should reinforce each
other.

### Intended workflow

1.  Research a system or a specific question.
2.  Organize the research into a system overview and a set of smaller
    questions.
3.  Produce concise short-form explainers from those questions.
4.  Publish the deeper, sourced version on the website.
5.  Link each social post to the relevant website page when possible.
6.  Update the website page when important facts change.
7.  Use audience questions to identify gaps in the library.

The short video is the entry point; the website is the durable knowledge
base.

### Practical constraints

The project may begin with limited production equipment and noisy
recording conditions. The website must not depend on polished video
production. It should stand on its own through good writing, diagrams,
sources, and organization. Do not make video the only way to consume
important information.

## 16. MVP scope: what the AI website-building agent should build first

The first version should be a functional, content-first website---not a
fully automated national knowledge platform.

### Required for the MVP

1.  Responsive homepage with the positioning and navigation described
    above.
2.  Explore Systems landing page.
3.  A reusable system-detail page template.
4.  Knowledge Library listing page.
5.  Reusable explainer/article template.
6.  About page with mission and editorial principles.
7.  Search, if supported by the chosen stack without disproportionate
    complexity.
8.  Source/reference section for content pages.
9.  Clear publication and update dates.
10. Related-content links.
11. Basic SEO metadata, sensible URLs, social sharing metadata, and
    sitemap support where applicable.
12. A maintainable way to add and update content.
13. Accessible responsive design and good loading performance.
14. Useful empty states where content is not yet available.
15. A clear way to contact the project or suggest a correction, once a
    working contact method is configured.

### Seed content

The prototype should include a small number of genuinely useful, clearly
labeled pages. Prefer: - one flagship Electricity system page; - one
Petrol/Fuel system page, if source material is ready; - a few short
explainers; - a small glossary or institutional profile only where the
content is researched.

If full research is not supplied to the build agent, use clearly marked
draft placeholders rather than inventing factual content. The visual
prototype can demonstrate the content model, but it must not make
fabricated statistics look real.

### Not required for the MVP

-   User accounts and profiles.
-   Comments or a public forum.
-   Paid memberships.
-   Advertising systems.
-   Complex CMS workflows.
-   AI chatbot.
-   Personalized recommendation algorithms.
-   Live dashboards for every public statistic.
-   Exhaustive coverage of every Pakistani institution.
-   A breaking-news ticker.
-   Heavy animation or a complex visual effects system.

The priority is to make the site structure excellent and content easy to
publish.

## 17. Technical and implementation guidance

The current prototype is deployed at:

https://pakistan-resolve.mehunhasnain.workers.dev/

The project has been deployed using Cloudflare's ecosystem. Preserve the
existing deployment path and project continuity where practical. Do not
assume the prototype's exact code structure or repository configuration
without inspecting it.

### Technical principles

-   Prefer a simple, maintainable stack over unnecessary complexity.
-   Keep content separate from presentation where practical.
-   Use reusable page templates and components.
-   Make it easy to add a system or explainer without redesigning the
    site.
-   Use stable, readable slugs such as `/systems/electricity` and
    `/explainers/how-electricity-billing-works`.
-   Include metadata for titles, descriptions, canonical URLs where
    appropriate, and social previews.
-   Optimize images and avoid heavy dependencies.
-   Support mobile devices and slow connections.
-   Use semantic HTML and accessible patterns.
-   Keep secrets and credentials out of client-side code.
-   Provide clear setup, content-editing, and deployment instructions.
-   Avoid building a database or backend unless the requirements
    actually justify one.

### Content storage options

Choose the simplest approach that meets the project needs: -
Markdown/MDX or structured files may be enough for an early, mostly
static library. - A headless CMS may be useful later if frequent
nontechnical editing becomes important. - A database should be
introduced only when content workflows, search, relationships, or scale
make it worthwhile.

Do not force a particular framework if the existing repository has a
sound approach. First inspect the current project, explain the proposed
changes briefly, and preserve working functionality unless there is a
strong reason to replace it.

## 18. Sustainability and future development

The immediate goal is to establish a useful, credible public resource.
Monetization should not dictate the editorial structure at this stage.

Possible future sustainability routes---only after the platform has
useful content and a real audience---could include grants, research
partnerships, educational products, carefully chosen sponsorships,
memberships, or services built around research and explainers. These are
possibilities, not commitments.

Any future funding model should preserve editorial independence and
clearly disclose relevant conflicts. Avoid designing the MVP around ad
placements or paywalls.

### Long-term possibilities

-   A larger searchable database of Pakistani systems and institutions.
-   Structured datasets with documented methodology.
-   Interactive flow diagrams and timelines.
-   Downloadable research briefs and educational resources.
-   Topic collections for students and researchers.
-   Urdu versions or bilingual content where sustainable.
-   Contributions from qualified researchers and subject experts.
-   A correction and community question workflow.

Do not implement these prematurely. Design the content model so that
they remain possible.

## 19. Measures of success

Early success is not measured only by traffic or social-media
impressions. Useful indicators include:

-   A new visitor can explain what Pakistan Resolve does after a few
    seconds on the homepage.
-   Readers can find the relevant system or explainer without confusion.
-   Pages have meaningful sources and dates.
-   The first system pages connect to related explanations instead of
    becoming isolated articles.
-   Content can be added without redesigning the site.
-   Visitors arrive from short-form content and can continue learning on
    the website.
-   Readers return to the site as a reference.
-   Corrections and updates can be made without friction.
-   The platform grows in depth and quality, not merely in the number of
    pages.

Later, track search terms, popular pages, referrals, reading engagement,
and unanswered visitor questions---but use analytics responsibly and
avoid intrusive tracking.

## 20. Key risks and how to avoid them

**Risk: Becoming a generic news blog.**\
Response: Make systems and evergreen explainers the primary
architecture. Keep recent posts as a secondary section.

**Risk: Too many categories and empty pages.**\
Response: Launch with a small number of populated systems and add
categories as research becomes available.

**Risk: Looking authoritative without being reliable.**\
Response: Make sources, dates, definitions, and uncertainty visible.
Never fabricate data.

**Risk: Overly academic or inaccessible writing.**\
Response: Start with a short answer and plain-language overview; provide
deeper detail below.

**Risk: Partisan capture.**\
Response: Apply consistent evidence standards to every party,
institution, and administration. Separate facts from analysis and
proposals.

**Risk: A beautiful site with no sustainable publishing workflow.**\
Response: Prioritize reusable templates, clear content fields, and easy
updates.

**Risk: Overengineering.**\
Response: Build the simplest architecture that supports connected
content and search.

**Risk: Treating AI as the source of truth.**\
Response: AI may help organize, draft, or transform research, but
factual claims and citations must be checked against actual sources.

**Risk: The project promises solutions before it has established a
knowledge base.**\
Response: Lead with explanation and evidence. Let practical proposals
emerge from careful research.

### The most important product test

Ask of every major design or architecture decision:

**"Does this help a person understand how Pakistan works, and does it
help connect that understanding to reliable evidence and related
knowledge?"**

If the answer is no, it is probably not a priority for the first
version.

## 22. One-paragraph brief for quick context

Pakistan Resolve is an independent Pakistan-focused knowledge hub with
the tagline "Awareness for Resolve." It began as a broad news and
public-awareness idea, but its evolved direction is to explain the
systems behind Pakistan's headlines: institutions, energy, economy,
public finance, governance, society, and public services. The website
should be a calm, accessible, research-oriented library organized around
systems and connected explainers, not a conventional breaking-news feed.
It should make complex subjects understandable, link claims to credible
sources, distinguish facts from analysis, and help readers move from a
quick explanation to deeper knowledge. Start with a focused MVP,
especially the Electricity system and the previously researched
Petrol/Fuel system where the source material is ready. Use a restrained
Pakistan-inspired visual identity, responsive design, reusable content
templates, useful search, and a maintainable publishing workflow. Do not
fabricate content or overbuild features. The goal is a durable public
knowledge resource that grows in depth, accuracy, and usefulness over
time.
