## How to read your own bill

You do not need any specialist knowledge to start. Take a recent bill and work through it:

1. **Find the consumption.** Note the units used in the billing period and the dates of the period.
2. **List every charge.** Write down each line with its amount and any label or code beside it.
3. **Separate the kinds of charge.** Group them into charges for electricity used, fixed charges, adjustments, and taxes or fees.
4. **Check the arithmetic.** Does the total equal the sum of the lines? Do any lines depend on how many units you used?
5. **Look up each label.** Find what each term means in the regulator's tariff documents.

## What stays the same and what changes

Some parts of a bill follow from rules that change rarely. Others change because of decisions taken during the year. A finished version of this page should say which is which, with dates, so a reader knows whether the explanation is current.

::: analysis
Many disputes about bills come from not knowing which charges are fixed and which are adjusted. A line-by-line guide is intended to reduce that confusion, not to argue for or against any charge.
:::
