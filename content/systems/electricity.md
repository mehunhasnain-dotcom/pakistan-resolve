## What the electricity system does

Electricity has to be produced at the moment it is used. A working power system therefore does four jobs at once: it generates power, moves it over long distances, delivers it to users, and collects enough money to pay for all of that. Problems in one job, such as unpaid bills or fuel costs, can show up somewhere else, such as outages or higher prices.

## How it works, step by step

The five stages in "The system at a glance" above describe a generic grid. For Pakistan, the questions to answer at each stage are:

1. **Generation:** Which kinds of plants supply the grid, who owns them, and under what kinds of contracts?
2. **Purchase and scheduling:** Who buys the electricity, and how is it decided which plants run at a given time?
3. **Transmission:** Who owns and operates the national grid, and how are its costs recovered?
4. **Distribution:** Which companies serve which regions, and how do they differ?
5. **Billing and collection:** How do charges reach the consumer, and what happens when bills go unpaid or electricity is lost or stolen along the way?
